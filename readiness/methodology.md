# Canonical Cloud pre-audit and readiness engagement method

Reviewed September 7, 2026. This is a proposed operating procedure for Canonical Cloud readiness work, not a replacement for an applicable audit standard, contractual examination procedure, regulator instruction or legal advice. Original catalog questions are broad intake prompts. A lead assessor must expand them into the complete, applicable control/requirement program before making a scoped readiness recommendation.

## 1. Establish the engagement and its limits

Record the customer legal entity, sponsor, assessment identifier, service/system and organizational boundaries, jurisdictions, locations, suppliers, data categories and evidence period. Define the decision the customer needs: gap discovery, examination preparation, an internal audit rehearsal, or remediation verification. State explicitly that the deliverable is a pre-audit/readiness assessment and identify who can perform the eventual independent examination, certification or authorization.

Obtain written permission for each kind of technical testing, listing owned/authorized targets, accounts, allowed operations, test windows, rate limits, emergency contacts, stop conditions and evidence handling. Customer module imports execute code: use disposable external isolation and restricted credentials even when a probe describes itself as read-only. A read-only collector is not a sandbox and a successful probe is not an audit conclusion.

Create a separate engagement/workbook for every selected framework. Do not auto-select a legal regime merely because a cloud service exists. Resolve whether the organization is in scope, what edition the contract requires, and which reporting/certification path is intended. A shared artifact may be separately referenced after review; an answer, N/A conclusion, sign-off or completion state is never inherited.

## 2. Approve a framework-specific assessment plan

For each applicable requirement, record the objective, expected evidence, responsible owner, chosen assessment methods, planned depth/coverage and reviewer. Use examination of documents/configurations, interviews with responsible people, and authorized tests as complementary methods. A policy is evidence of an intended design; an interview alone does not demonstrate that a control operated throughout a reporting period. Specify what operating samples or observed tests are necessary.

NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5 supplies the current assessment-procedure foundation for its associated control catalog. NIST's older worked assessment cases illustrate examine/interview/test and depth/coverage concepts, but explicitly correspond to earlier revisions; do not substitute their old control text for the current program. Sources: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/a/r5/final and https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/risk-management/about-rmf/assess-step/assessment-cases-overview .

Create the detailed control list from the applicable licensed/public authority, not from guessed crosswalk mappings. For each exclusion or N/A, document the factual basis, applicable scope and reviewer decision. An unavailable artifact is not an exclusion. Keep the original scope and subsequent changes versioned; additional scope requires a revised plan and authorized testing boundaries.

## 3. Collect evidence with traceable provenance

Use an evidence request register with an opaque identifier, requested requirement, owner, due date, purpose and acceptable forms. Keep artifacts in an access-controlled repository rather than in questionnaire notes. Record collection time, source system, observation period, collector/tool version, read-only permissions used, relevant configuration, cryptographic digest where appropriate and any redaction or transformation. Hashes support integrity checks but do not prove truth, lawful collection or anonymization.

Review evidence for relevance to the actual system and period, completeness of the population, authenticity indicators, and consistency with other artifacts. A screenshot of today's configuration does not prove last quarter's operation. Verify changed configurations and stale evidence rather than applying an unexplained universal age threshold. Treat absence, inaccessible exports, failed collectors and truncated probes as limitations/unknowns until investigated. A discovered exception should be supported by a reproducible workpaper, not simply by a heuristic match.

Minimize personal data, credentials and health information. Apply the agreed legal basis, access restrictions, retention schedule and deletion/return process to the evidence itself. Do not feed raw evidence to an AI service without customer authorization, an approved processing arrangement and controls appropriate to its sensitivity. Treat any document or log instructions as untrusted content; AI summaries are drafts linked to evidence IDs, not independent findings or legal conclusions.

## 4. Design and record sampling rather than guessing a sample count

Define the complete population for a control and period: for example, production deployments, leavers, privileged grants, restore attempts or incident tickets. Record the population source/query, reconciliation of completeness, selection method, risk strata, dates and exact selected items. Choose coverage based on the applicable audit methodology, control frequency, risk, intended assurance and population characteristics. Obtain the lead assessor's approval; there is no universal sample size supplied by this tool.

Avoid cherry-picked customer examples as the sole basis for an operating-effectiveness conclusion. Record missing items, substitutions and their reasons. Separate a design walkthrough from an operating sample. When exceptions appear, investigate their extent, record any additional sampling and explain how it affects the conclusion. For zero-event populations, document why there were no events and test the relevant process through an authorized scenario where appropriate; do not manufacture historical evidence.

## 5. Conduct framework-specific work, not a merged questionnaire

**SOC 2:** agree the system description, trust-services categories, service commitments, report type and intended period with the CPA. Make complementary customer/subservice responsibilities explicit. Readiness workpapers do not become a SOC report. Sources: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022 and https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/get-description-criteria-for-your-organizations-soc-2-r-report .

**NIST CSF 2.0:** document the organization's Current Profile, desired Target Profile and a prioritized improvement plan. Do not present a Profile/Tier or generic percentage as a NIST-issued certification. Source: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/profiles . **SP 800-53 and SP 800-171:** select the applicable tailored baseline/contractual edition and assessment procedures separately. The Rev. 3 800-171 workbook does not silently satisfy a Rev. 2 or CMMC-specific contractual requirement. Sources: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/a/r5/final and https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/171/a/r3/final .

**ISO/IEC 27001:** assess the scoped ISMS, risk-treatment/Statement-of-Applicability decisions, operational evidence, internal audit, management review and corrective actions against the licensed standard, including applicable amendments. **27701** and **42001** are distinct management-system engagements, not completed by an ISMS answer. The catalog pins 27701:2025 rather than the withdrawn 2019 edition. Sources: https://www.iso.org/standard/27001 , https://www.iso.org/standard/27701 and https://www.iso.org/standard/42001 .

**GDPR:** determine territorial scope, controller/processor roles, purposes and lawful bases, rights processes, processors, transfers, retention, security and breach handling. Investigate whether DPIA/DPO requirements apply rather than answering universally. Privacy counsel should resolve legal interpretations. Sources: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng and https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme_en .

**HIPAA:** identify regulated roles and all scoped ePHI locations before evaluating safeguards. HHS risk-analysis guidance requires a thorough analysis and explains that an addressable specification is not simply optional: document the reasoned decision and applicable equivalent measures. Include Privacy and Breach Notification obligations where applicable; a security scan alone is insufficient. Sources: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/guidance-risk-analysis/index.html and https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html . Proposed Security Rule changes are tracked separately from enforceable requirements.

**PCI DSS:** validate payment scope, segmentation and the acquirer/brand-required validation path before collecting requirement-level evidence. Use the official applicable SAQ/ROC documents where required; the original Canonical questions are neither. Source: https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/ . **CIS Controls:** choose and justify the Implementation Group and apply the relevant assessment specification; a broad intake answer is not safeguard-level proof. Source: https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/cis-controls-assessment-specification . **CSA CCM:** use v4.1 and explicit provider/customer responsibility allocation; do not describe this ten-question intake as the official CAIQ or a STAR certification. Source: https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/cloud-controls-matrix-v4-1 .

**NIS2 and DORA:** independently determine entity scope, jurisdictions, national implementation or sector-specific technical obligations and the appropriate competent authorities. Proposed policy revisions must not be silently treated as enacted duties. Sources: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive and https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/digital-operational-resilience-act-dora_en . **FedRAMP Rev5:** verify the current program path and eligibility; the formal Ready designation requires the applicable recognized-assessor process and is not federal authorization. This intake does not create a RAR, Ready listing, 20x validation or ATO. Source: https://www.fedramp.gov/rev5/agency-authorization/ .

## 6. Produce findings, remediation and an independently reviewed handoff

Maintain distinct outcomes: not assessed/unknown, supported implementation, declared or verified gap, and justified N/A. Separate customer declarations from assessor conclusions. For an actual finding, record the requirement/objective, evidence IDs, observed condition, impact/risk, scope, owner, corrective action, target date and retest criteria. Do not inflate a missing artifact into a proven technical failure; do not mark a control effective merely because a filename was supplied.

Hold a factual-accuracy review with the customer without letting management overwrite the assessor's workpaper history. Resolve contradictory evidence and record residual uncertainty. Retest corrective actions with new dated evidence, preserving original findings and the review trail. Distinguish immediate configuration fixes from controls requiring a sustained period of operation. Record risk acceptance separately from remediation; acceptance does not erase a contractual or legal obligation.

The final readiness packet should contain the agreed scope and limitations, edition/applicability decisions, separate framework workbooks, assessment plan, evidence index, sampling record, workpaper index, findings/remediation register, retest evidence, unresolved requests and lead-reviewer sign-off. Explain what has actually been tested and what remains unverified. Any recommendation to proceed to an external audit is a scoped professional judgment, not a machine-generated certificate. Hand over to the appropriately qualified independent assessor without implying Canonical Cloud has issued their opinion.

## Operational release gates for this implementation

The shipped workbook and CLI automate intake, input validation, completeness/gap summaries and Markdown/JSON export. They do not verify evidence content, customer declarations, reviewer identity, legal applicability or operating effectiveness. Before expanding to durable customer evidence storage, require authenticated ownership, server-side authorization/RLS tests, access auditing, encryption, retention/deletion controls and threat-model review. Before making formal audit-readiness claims, require framework-specific expert review of the full control program and the particular customer's evidence.
